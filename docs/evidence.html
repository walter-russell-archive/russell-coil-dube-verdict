From The Archives

Editors note: Royal Lee was a dentist, inventor, and pioneer in nutrition. He founded Standard Process laboratories

(continuation, journal p. 16 foot:)

which still manufactures natural whole food concentrates. The Russell's, especially Lao Russell, had a common interest in natural approaches to health, healing and whole natural foods diet with Royal Lee.

Document 2 — Walter Russell to Dr. Royal Lee (journal p. 16 / PDF 19)

March 20th 1954.
Dear Dr. Lee:
I cannot tell you how pleased my wife (Lao) and I were to
receive a copy of the Tesla book which you reprinted for the benefit
of thinkers in science. Nicola Tesla and I exchanged inspirations for
many years. He was an artist at heart whom the world knew as a scientist-
while I was a scientist at heart whom the world knew as an artist.
He was my spiritual mainstay while I produced my greatest
work in sculpture - The Mark Twain Memorial, and I likewise spiritually
supported when his soul was low from doubtings and attacks by lesser
minds.
I give you these reasons why I especially thank you for the
book-- but I also add an appreciation for your vision which indicates a
love and admiration for him such as had grown in me.
I could never understand why Nicola Tesla was not proclaimed
the greatest man in science of his day - for he was just that and your
gesture in printing that book makes me feel deeply indebted to you in
my friend's name.
Thinking you may be interested to know of our work here -
and my unrewarded contributions to science which have received even
less recognition than Nicola's - having given the six transuranium
elements to the world as you will see by the enclosed charts - I enclose
a little booklet of my life and my second open letter to science for
your perusal.
Mrs. Russell joins me in extending to you a cordial invitation
to visit our mountain top shrine at any time you may feel like looking at
the other side of the medal upon which is written the constitution of
matter from Nature's point of view to replace the unnatural one written
on the visible side by man.
Thanking you again and looking forward to a interchange of
thoughts whenever you feel so inclined, we extend to you our good wishes
and greetings.
Sincerely yours
Walter Russell.
P. S. You ask if I might wish to send one to any other Tesla admirer.
[Dr?] John O'Neill, Science editor of the Herald Tribune, wrote an excellent
book of Tesla's life, entitled PRODIGAL GENIUS of which I have an
autographed copy.  I am sure he would be delighted to exchange copies
with you, and I am sure you would like his book.

Editorial notes

  • Chronology: the printed order is reversed. Russell's letter (Mar 20, 1954) thanks Lee for the reprinted Tesla book. Lee's letter (Jan 6, 1955) answers a later Russell letter of Jan 2 [1955], which requested a further copy of the Tesla book for a Mr. Maurice Miller. The "Tesla book" Lee reprinted is very likely John J. O'Neill's Prodigal Genius (mentioned in Russell's P.S.) or a Tesla lecture reprint. The letters do not name the reprint's title.
  • Bearing on the "Tesla told Russell to bury his knowledge for 1,000 years" folklore: these letters neither state nor allude to any such instruction. Russell himself characterizes the relationship as mutual inspiration and emotional support ("Nicola Tesla and I exchanged inspirations for many years", "He was my spiritual mainstay… I likewise spiritually supported when his soul was low"). No mention of burying, sealing, or withholding knowledge appears anywhere. Moreover, the 1996 USP editor states flatly that this letter "is the only item in the archives that shows any trace of Walter Russell and Nikola Tesla's association". He also appeals to readers for any actual correspondence. That is, as of 1996 the USP archive held no documentary support for the folklore quote. The letters therefore undercut the legend's documentary basis. They prove acquaintance, but the famous "bury it" line rests on no archived Tesla document. Notably, in the coil correspondence three years later (journal p. 21), Russell argues the opposite of secrecy: "the great secrecy which we thought necessary was not at all necessar[y]".
  • Russell's claim of "having given the six transuranium elements to the world" refers to his 1926 periodic-chart predictions. His spelling "Nicola" (for Nikola) is as typed.
  • The illegible handwritten note on Lee's letter appears to list enclosures (a malnutrition pamphlet and a reprint list), consistent with "I enclose a copy of a lecture".
Gap in the record — August 8, 1958: Walter Russell's coil description. Dube's letter of August 25 answers a Russell "description dated August 8th". Neither issue reprinted that description. It survives, if at all, in USP's private archives.

Dear Dr. Russell:
The conventional method of constructing a solenoid valve is illus-
trated in Fig. 31, page 64 of our Handbook, a copy of which is
enclosed. The armature moves up and down in a cylindrical cavity
in the center of the coil winding. Since the Russell Coil has no
through center cavity, I do not see how we can directly compare
its efficiency to our standard units. As an alternate we suggest
making two pull test fixtures as illustrated in Figs. 1 and 3.
Referring to Fig. 1, Parts (1) and (2) are identical halves of the
iron core, shaped as suggested in your description dated August 8th.
The cross sectional area of the iron in any section perpendicular
to the axis will be consistent except at transition (or step) points.
Parts (6) and (11) are flat iron straps to "conduct the energy" to
an armature assembly. Bolt (3) holds the straps and iron core
halves together and is made of non-magnetic material. Part (7) is
a magnetic top plug which is bolted to the other end of strap (6).
Enclosing tube (8), which is made of non-magnetic material and is
pre-welded or brazed to the top plug, serves as a guide for the
magnetic armature (9) that is free to slide vertically in the tube.
Part (10) is a cylindrical, magnetic bushing that fits on the out-
side of the enclosing tube and on the inside of a hole in magnetic
strap (11). The bushing provides an enlarged flow path for the
energy between the strap (11) and the armature (9), thus tending
to reduce the flow resistance through the "non-conducting" tube
(8) and the small air gap between the tube (8) and the armature
(9). Sleeve (4) is an insulating tube on which to wind the copper
wire (5). A separate sleeve will be used for each section of the
winding.

Editorial notes

  • This letter is a direct reply to Walter Russell's coil "description dated August 8th" (1958). This issue does not reprint that description.
  • The test protocol described here is a constant 5-watt input with a measured breakaway pull force. Its results appear in Fig. 4 (journal p. 92). Fig. 4, though, records the run conditions as 24 VDC / 4.4 watts.

Editorial notes

  • This letter replies to a Dube letter "of the 3rd inst." (October 3, 1958), which this issue does not reprint. That Dube letter evidently transmitted the revised test-unit drawings LO 8308-11 / LO 8308-12 dated Oct 2, 1958 (journal pp. 93–94). REPORT ON EVALUATION OF RUSSELL COIL
[OBJ]ECT OF PROGRAM:
To compare the pulling power of the Russell
type solenoid coil with conventional coils.
[DE]SIGN OF COILS:
Two Russell coils were constructed in accord
with the layout, Drawing L08308-11 and asso-
ciated detail parts drawings.  Two conventional
coils were constructed in accord with Drawing
L08308-12 and associated detail parts drawings.
Prints of all above drawings are attached.  The
sizes of the various coil windings were calcu-
lated to use the same total length of copper
wire per unit.  The sum of the resistances of
the segments of the Russell coil actually came
out to be 4% less than the conventional coil.
This small difference is less than the deviation
we allow in production of conventional coils so
is considered acceptable.  The difference favors
the Russell coil, so is not detrimental in this
evaluation.
[DE]SCRIPTION OF
[TE]ST #1:
Two completed conventional solenoids were mounted
in line axially with a one-half inch gap between
the adjacent core buttons.  The windings were
wired as indicated in Fig. 1 and 24 volts direct
current power was applied to the leads.  Iron
filings were dropped into the 1/2 inch gap between
buttons until the solenoids were apparently sup-
porting the maximum amount of filings.  This amount
was weighed.  The test was repeated using Russell
coils wired as shown in Fig. 2.  There was no
apparent difference in the supporting power of the
Russell and conventional coils.  However the test
was considered inconclusive because the operator
could vary the supporting power by packing the
filings around the buttons, leading to the suspicion
that the method of application of the filing might
make more difference in the test results than the
difference between the coils being compared.  This
form of testing was abandoned in favor of the fol-
lowing test.

Journal p. 19 (PDF 22)

[DE]SCRIPTION OF
[TE]ST #2:
Two conventional coils were wired as indicated
in Fig. 1 and mounted in the test fixture shown
in Fig. 3.  With 24 volts applied to the leads
the pull necessary to separate the two coils was
measured by means of a sensitive force scale.
The test was repeated a number of times with dif-
ferent thicknesses of plastic spacers creating a
gap between the buttons.  The same procedure was
followed with the Russell solenoid wired as shown
in Fig. 2.  All tests were repeated a number of
times with very consistent results.  With no gap
(spacers removed) the conventional solenoid drew
4.4 watts and the Russell coil 4.6 watts.
[RE]SULTS:
The attached Fig. 4 is a plot of the pulling power
of each type coil against the gap.  The conventional
coil has 5 to 10% more pulling power throughout the
range of gaps tested.
[CO]NCLUSIONS:
(1) The slight inferiority of pulling power of the
    Russell solenoid can be accounted for by conventional
    theory.  Since both type coils had the same length
    of copper wire in the windings, and since the average
    diameter of the Russell coil was slightly larger (due
    to the irregular core shape and loss of space between
    the segments of the winding) the Russell coil has
    slightly fewer turns, and this generates fewer
    ampere-turns of magnetic power, when operated at the
    same amperage and wattage as conventional designs.
(2) Since the Russell design inherently loses winding
    space it would appear to be inferior rather than
    superior to conventional design.
(3) Even if some new winding technique were developed
    to overcome the space loss in the Russell coil, it
    would appear that it would equal but not exceed
    conventional designs.

Editorial notes

  • Referenced but not reprinted: the report cites wiring diagrams Figs. 1–2, test fixture Fig. 3, and the pulling-power-vs-gap plot Fig. 4. It also cites layout drawings L08308-11 (Russell coil) / L08308-12 (conventional coil) with "associated detail parts drawings." The report says all of these are attached. None of these figures or drawings appear anywhere in this Fulcrum issue. We checked through PDF page 30 (journal p. 27). After journal p. 23 the issue switches to an unrelated 1923 "Russell Rent Mutualization Plan" reprint. Russell's P.S. of Nov. 24, 1958 asked Dube to keep copies and return the originals, so the drawings presumably went back to Swannanoa.
  • Fig. 4 recovered in V5N1: Fulcrum Vol. 5, No. 1 reprints the pull-test graph (dated ~Nov 17, 1958, 24 VDC / 4.4 W), along with drawings LO 8308-11/-12 and detail parts LO 8308-101..-109 and -120..-122 (see transcripts/INDEX_v5n1.md). A calibrated read of that graph (±0.05 lb, gridline-calibrated; see schematics/README_schematics.md) gives four points per curve at gaps 0/.014/.028/.040 in: standard 5.09/2.62/1.72/1.32 lb, step 4.80/2.38/1.52/1.21 lb. The conventional-coil advantage is 6% at contact, 10% and 13% at the middle gaps, and 9% at .040 in. This matches the report's stated "5 to 10% more pulling power throughout the range of gaps tested" within reading uncertainty. An earlier approximate read of the graph endpoints (~2.1 vs ~1.4 lb, ≈50% spread) was a misread, and this note supersedes it. A power cross-check corroborates the data: 24²/131 Ω = 4.40 W and 24²/126 Ω = 4.57 W, which equal the report's 4.4 W and 4.6 W figures.
  • Methodological quality: for 1958 industrial practice this is a clean controlled comparison. The units have equal copper length. The resistances match within production tolerance, and the 4% deviation favors the Russell coil — i.e., it biases the test toward the claim under test. The staff candidly flagged the operator-dependence of the abandoned first protocol. They repeated the trials and swept the gap. The confound they identify in Conclusion (1) (larger mean winding diameter → fewer turns → fewer ampere-turns) is textbook solenoid theory.
  • Bottom line: the conventional coil is 5–10% stronger across all gaps. The Russell coil drew slightly more power at zero gap (4.6 W vs 4.4 W). That is, the Russell coil is worse on both counts.

Editorial notes

  • The key concession (p. 1, ¶4–5): the Russells accept the null result unreservedly — "any two wire coils, wound in any way whatsoever, will give equal resultant field potential, providing that the various dimensions of each coil are equal. We can readily see that now, and can admit it as a fact of law, as you have proved it to be." The 1996 editor quotes this sentence on journal p. 23.
  • The reframe: the letter concedes the effect (greater power). It then pivots to claim that the coil's real purpose was always to "control the construction of matter" — transmutation, not output — and it reclassifies the power claim as "purely theoretical and unimportant." The comparisons to Bell and Goodyear, the geopolitical stakes (GOOD vs evil, missile immunity), and the Westinghouse water-transmutation story all serve this pivot.
  • Secrecy reversal: the test convinced them that "the great secrecy which we thought necessary was not at all necessar[y]". This is directly relevant to community folklore about Russell's secret knowledge. Here Russell himself says they could give the coil to anyone safely.
  • "whichwould", "andwater", "laws,for", "theroetical", "toneSof" are run-together or slipped-key typos preserved as typed in the original. "greatgiver" is one word in the original. The extra space in "obstacle as" (p. 3) reflects the typing.
  • The letter treats Maguire's withdrawal as an accomplished fact ("you are fully justified in withdrawing from it"). No further Alco/Maguire test round is known from this issue.
